023: The Zel'dovich Approximation & The Cosmic Web

An elegant model that reveals how the filamentary structure of the universe is encoded in its initial conditions.

Overview for the Curious

While full N-body simulations are powerful, a surprisingly elegant and simple approximation developed by Soviet physicist Yakov Zel'dovich provides deep insight into why the universe is structured like a "cosmic web." The **Zel'dovich approximation** realizes that in the early universe, before gravity has had time to get complicated, the future positions of particles are encoded in their initial velocities. It's like knowing the starting speed and direction of every car in a traffic jam; you can predict where the pile-ups will happen. This simulation starts with particles on a uniform grid and then displaces them according to a random, primordial velocity field. As you increase the "time" slider, you are not watching a complex gravity simulation, but simply watching particles move in straight lines. The astonishing result is that this simple process naturally forms a network of dense sheets, filaments, and nodes—a perfect skeleton of the cosmic web.
