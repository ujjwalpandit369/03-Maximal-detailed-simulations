011: Visualizing Anti-de Sitter Spacetime

An interactive exploration of the strange, beautiful, and confining geometry of AdS space using the Poincaré Disk model.

Poincaré Disk View of AdS₂

Overview for the Curious

Imagine a universe contained within a giant, perfectly reflecting fishbowl. From the inside, this universe would seem infinite. As you tried to travel to the edge, your steps would seem to get smaller and smaller, and you would never reach it. This is a way to think about **Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space**. It's a specific type of curved spacetime predicted by Einstein's theory of relativity that has a "boundary" infinitely far away. This simulation uses the **Poincaré Disk**—a famous mathematical map—to visualize this strange universe. The entire infinite AdS space is mapped to the interior of the circle you see. You can fire particles and light rays (photons) and watch them travel along **geodesics** (the "straight lines" in this curved space). You'll discover that AdS acts like a gravitational box: massive particles can't escape and will oscillate back and forth as if held by a giant spring. This confining property is what makes AdS space a crucial theoretical laboratory for studying quantum gravity and black holes.
