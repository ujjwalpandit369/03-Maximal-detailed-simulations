013: The Hawking-Page Phase Transition

A thermodynamic showdown between a gas of thermal radiation and a massive black hole in Anti-de Sitter space.

Free Energy (F) vs. Temperature (T)

Overview for the Curious

In nature, systems tend to settle into their state of lowest **free energy**. Think of a ball rolling downhill to find the bottom of a valley. The same principle applies to the entire universe. In the confining "box" of Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space, there are two possible states the universe can be in at a high temperature: a uniform "soup" of hot radiation (a **thermal gas**), or a single, massive **black hole** in equilibrium with its own radiation. Which one is more stable? This simulation plots the free energy of both states as a function of temperature. You will discover that at low temperatures, the gas is more stable. But as you increase the temperature past a critical point—the **Hawking-Page transition temperature**—the free energy of the black hole state suddenly drops lower. The universe undergoes a **phase transition**, like water boiling into steam, and it becomes thermodynamically favorable for the gas to collapse and form a black hole. This is a profound link between gravity, thermodynamics, and quantum field theory.
