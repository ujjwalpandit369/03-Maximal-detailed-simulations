022: Gravitational Collapse of an Overdensity

Witness the birth of the first cosmic structures as gravity battles against the expansion of the universe.

N-body Simulation in an Expanding Box

Energy and Virial Ratio vs. Time

Overview for the Curious

The early universe was remarkably smooth and uniform, but not perfectly so. Quantum fluctuations in the very first moments of time created tiny regions that were slightly denser than average. This simulation shows what happens to one such **overdensity**. Initially, everything expands due to the Hubble flow. But the overdense region has extra gravity. This extra pull acts as a brake, slowing the expansion of the region. If the overdensity is strong enough, it can halt the expansion completely, "turn around," and begin to **collapse** under its own weight. This process of gravitational instability is the primary engine of all structure formation, transforming the smooth early universe into the rich cosmic web of galaxies and clusters we see today. You can also watch the system's energy evolve and see it satisfy the **Virial Theorem**, a key indicator that a stable, bound object has formed.
