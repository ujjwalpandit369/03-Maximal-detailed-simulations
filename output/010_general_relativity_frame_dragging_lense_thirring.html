010: Frame-Dragging & The Lense-Thirring Effect

Visualizing how a spinning mass twists the very fabric of spacetime around it.

Spacetime Dragging Effect

Overview for the Curious

Imagine a heavy bowling ball spinning in a tub of thick honey. As it spins, it will drag the honey around with it, creating a swirl. According to Einstein's General Relativity, a massive, spinning object like the Earth or a black hole does the exact same thing to spacetime itself. This phenomenon is called **frame-dragging**, or the **Lense-Thirring effect**. It means that spacetime is not a passive backdrop, but an active medium that can be twisted and dragged. This simulation shows the consequences of this effect. A satellite in a polar orbit doesn't trace the same path over and over. Instead, the entire orbital plane is slowly dragged around by the central body's rotation. This effect is minuscule but was famously measured by the Gravity Probe B satellite, confirming one of the most bizarre and wonderful predictions of Einstein's theory.
