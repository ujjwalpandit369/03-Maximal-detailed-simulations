Overview for the Curious

In the hot, dense early universe, photons (light) and baryons (normal matter like hydrogen) were locked together in a hot, ionized plasma. This plasma was so dense that it behaved like a fluid that could carry sound waves. An initial overdensity of matter, created by inflation, would act like a stone dropped in this cosmic pond, generating a spherical **sound wave** that expanded outwards. This simulation shows what happens. The dark matter, which doesn't feel the plasma's pressure, stays in the center. The sound wave in the photon-baryon fluid travels outwards for 380,000 years. At that point, the universe cools enough for atoms to form (**recombination**), the photons are released, and the sound wave stalls. It leaves behind a shell of slightly overdense baryonic matter at a very specific distance from the original dark matter clump. This characteristic distance, the **sound horizon**, is imprinted on the cosmic web, creating a slight preference for galaxies to be separated by about 500 million light-years today. This "BAO feature" is a powerful "standard ruler" that cosmologists use to measure the expansion of the universe.
