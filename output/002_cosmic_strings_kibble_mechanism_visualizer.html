Overview for Lay Readers

Imagine a field of tiny, spinning compasses. At high temperatures, they all spin randomly. As you cool them, they want to align with their neighbors. But since there's no global "north," they form swirling patches where compasses point in similar, slowly changing directions. At the center of some of these swirls, you'll find a point of total confusion where the compass direction is undefined. This point is a defect, an analog for a cosmic string. This simulation visualizes a similar process for a fundamental field in the early universe. The "direction" of the field is shown as a color. The points where all colors meet are the cosmic strings—incredibly thin, massive threads of trapped energy left over from a cosmic phase transition.
