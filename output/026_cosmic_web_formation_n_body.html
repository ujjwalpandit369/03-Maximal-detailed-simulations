Overview for the Curious

On the largest scales, the universe resembles a gigantic, glowing spiderweb. Galaxies are not scattered randomly, but are arranged in long, delicate **filaments** and dense **nodes**, which are separated by vast, empty **voids**. This beautiful, complex structure emerged from a nearly uniform early universe through the relentless pull of gravity. This simulation is a large-scale N-body simulation that lets you watch the formation of the **cosmic web** in action. It starts with thousands of particles distributed according to the primordial fluctuations measured from the Cosmic Microwave Background. As time progresses, you can see matter draining from the voids and streaming along the filaments to collect in the nodes, forming the first galaxy clusters. This simulation uses a more advanced **Particle-Mesh (PM)** method to handle the large number of particles efficiently, providing a glimpse into the techniques used in modern cosmological research.
