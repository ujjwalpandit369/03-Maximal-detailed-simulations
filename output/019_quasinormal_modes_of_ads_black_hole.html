Overview for the Curious

If you strike a bell, it rings with a specific tone and the sound fades away over time. A black hole does the same thing with gravity. If a black hole is disturbed—for example, by swallowing a star or merging with another black hole—the newly formed horizon is misshapen and unstable. It quickly settles down to a perfect sphere, shedding the excess energy and distortion in a burst of gravitational waves. This "ringdown" phase is not random noise; it's a clear, pure tone, like a bell. The frequencies and damping times of this gravitational-wave sound are called **quasinormal modes (QNMs)**. They are the unique "fingerprint" of a black hole, depending only on its mass, spin, and charge. This simulation lets you "poke" a black hole in AdS space and watch the resulting wave travel, reflect, and get measured by a detector, revealing the beautiful damped sinusoidal signal of the black hole's fundamental ringdown mode.
