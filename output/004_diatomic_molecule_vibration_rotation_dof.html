Overview for the Curious

A molecule, like two balls connected by a spring, can do more than just move from place to place. It can tumble end-over-end (**rotation**) and the springy bond between its atoms can stretch and compress (**vibration**). In the quantum world, a molecule can't just have *any* amount of rotational or vibrational energy; it can only have specific, discrete amounts, creating a ladder of "energy levels". This simulation shows the classical motion of a two-atom molecule and simultaneously visualizes the corresponding quantum energy levels. By understanding these internal degrees of freedom, we can understand how molecules absorb light at very specific frequencies, creating a unique "barcode" or **spectrum** that allows scientists to identify them across the vastness of space.

A Bridge to the Quantum World

This simulation is explicitly "semi-classical". The motion of the atoms is calculated using Newton's laws, which are purely classical. However, the analysis and visualization are based on the principles of quantum mechanics. We calculate the classical energy of rotation and vibration and then find the nearest allowed quantum energy levels, `v` (vibrational) and `J` (rotational). This approach provides powerful intuition. For example, you will see that to jump to a higher vibrational level requires a large, specific "kick" of energy, while changing rotational levels is much easier. This is why vibrational transitions in spectroscopy are major events, each accompanied by a fine structure of many smaller rotational transitions. This simulation lets you see the classical motion that corresponds to these quantum labels.
