030: Gravitational Lensing by the Cosmic Web

Using the distortion of distant galaxy images to "see" the invisible dark matter scaffolding of the universe.

Lensed View of Background Galaxies

Overview for the Curious

How do you map what you cannot see? This is the central challenge in studying dark matter. The answer is to use **gravitational lensing**. According to Einstein, massive objects bend the path of light, acting like a lens. The invisible dark matter in the cosmic web acts as a giant, imperfect lens, distorting our view of the galaxies behind it. This simulation demonstrates this effect. It starts with a foreground "lens plane" containing dark matter halos and filaments, and a background "source plane" of perfectly circular galaxies. It then traces the light from those background galaxies to us, bending the paths as they pass through the dark matter. The result is a distorted view of the background galaxies; they are slightly stretched and sheared into elliptical shapes. The pattern of this shearing directly traces the location of the invisible dark matter, allowing astronomers to create a map of the cosmic web.
