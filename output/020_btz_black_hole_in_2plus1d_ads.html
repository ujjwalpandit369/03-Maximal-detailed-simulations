020: The BTZ Black Hole in (2+1) Dimensions

A simplified "toy model" universe that unlocks the deep secrets of black hole thermodynamics and holography.

Geodesics in BTZ Spacetime (Poincaré Disk)

Overview for the Curious

What if the universe had only two spatial dimensions, like a flat sheet? In this (2+1)-dimensional world, Einstein's theory of gravity has a surprisingly simple and elegant solution for a black hole: the **BTZ black hole**. Unlike its 4D cousins, which are complex objects, the BTZ black hole has the same local geometry as empty Anti-de Sitter space; it's only its global structure that is different, as if it were "cut and pasted" from empty space. Despite its simplicity, it has a temperature, entropy, and event horizon, making it a perfect theoretical laboratory. Physicists use it to test the wildest ideas about quantum gravity, black hole evaporation, and the holographic principle in a setting where the calculations can often be done exactly. This simulation lets you explore the geometry of this toy universe and see how its thermodynamics provide a powerful check on the AdS/CFT correspondence.
