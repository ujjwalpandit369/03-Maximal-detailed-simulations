Overview for the Curious

Galaxies like our own Milky Way are not isolated islands in space. They are embedded within vast, invisible spheres of **dark matter** called **halos**. These halos are the true building blocks of the cosmos, acting as the gravitational anchors for all visible matter. But how do these halos themselves grow? This simulation demonstrates the two primary mechanisms. The first is **smooth accretion**, where the halo's powerful gravity steadily pulls in a gentle rain of surrounding dark matter particles. The second is **mergers**, where the halo collides with and consumes other, smaller halos. This process is much more violent and dramatic. By watching the simulation and the plot of mass versus time, you can see how the steady, slow ramp of accretion is punctuated by the sudden, sharp jumps of mergers, revealing the "hierarchical" or "bottom-up" way our universe was built.
